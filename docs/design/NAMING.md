## 1. Brief and naming criteria

**Product.** An evidence-based hiring platform. Every resume claim sits on the **Evidence Ladder** (self-claimed → resume-stated → corroborated → demonstrated → interview-verified). Verification agents check GitHub/GitLab code, portfolio sites, documents, Open Badges and references. They cite every source, and a recruiter confirms each result. Review is blind-first, and the platform produces fairness reports.

**Audience.** HR teams and recruiters at companies (the buyers), and candidates (who need to trust the product too).

**Criteria used to judge names**
1. **Means something true about the product.** Proof, a standard, traceability or the ladder. It should not just sound like a tech brand.
2. **Calm and professional.** No gimmick suffixes ("-ify", "-ly", "AI"), and no "Hire-" or "Recruit-" prefix.
3. **Works for both audiences.** A candidate should not feel judged by the name.
4. **Easy to say, spell and remember.** It should work in a sentence ("we ran it through ___").
5. **Clear of the field.** No same or confusingly similar name in HR tech, recruiting, background checks, identity checks or developer tools.
6. **Gives the visual identity something to work with.** A shape for the logo and a story for the motion.

## 4. The three finalists

All three share these product rules, which the current UI already follows:
- **Verdicts never rely on colour alone.** Every chip has a symbol (✓ ◐ ○ ! –) and a text label.
- **"Not found" is neutral.** It uses grey, never red or amber, and never wording that sounds negative.
- **Tiers are ordinal, verdicts are semantic.** Evidence tiers use a single-hue ramp that gets stronger as evidence gets stronger, with a filled-rung count. Verdicts use green, amber and red meanings. The two systems never share a hue, so a "Contradicted" chip can't be confused with a "low tier".
- **Tier 1 (self-claimed) is drawn hollow** (outline only). Tiers 2 to 5 are filled. All tier fills reach at least 3:1 against card surfaces (WCAG 1.4.11, non-text contrast).
- Contrast ratios below were calculated with the WCAG 2.x relative-luminance formula. AA means 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text, UI borders and graphics.
- Motion: every animation has a `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback (instant, or an opacity fade of 120 ms or less). No looping animation, no animated gradients, no parallax. Nothing shakes or flashes when a claim is contradicted.

### 4.1 Plumbline (recommended)

**Pronunciation:** PLUM-line /ˈplʌm.laɪn/ (the *b* is silent).

**Meaning and story.** A plumb line is a weight on a string. Gravity pulls it to true vertical, so it gives builders a standard that doesn't depend on anyone's opinion. Plumbline does the same for hiring: every claim is held against the same line of cited evidence, whoever the candidate is.

**Tagline:** *Every claim, measured true.*
Alternates: "Hire against a true line." / "Evidence you can stand behind."

**Conflict notes:** see section 3. No HR, identity-check or developer-tool conflict found. The old Class 35/42 registration is cancelled. Search results are dominated by plumbing trades, which hurts SEO early on. The .com is probably held.

**Domain ideas** (availability not checked; nothing bought): `plumbline.work`, `useplumbline.com`, `getplumbline.com`, `plumblinehq.com`, `plumbline.hr` (check the .hr eligibility rules first).

#### Logo mark: "the line and the bob"
SVG `viewBox="0 0 32 32"`, drawn on a 32-unit grid:
1. **Anchor bar:** `<rect x="8" y="3" width="16" height="3" rx="1.5"/>`, fill `--ink` (neutral-900 in light, neutral-50 in dark).
2. **Line:** `<line x1="16" y1="6" x2="16" y2="19" stroke-width="2" stroke-linecap="butt"/>`, stroke `--ink`.
3. **Bob:** `<path d="M16 29.5 L10.5 19 H21.5 Z" stroke-width="1" stroke-linejoin="round"/>`, an inverted triangle 11 wide and 10.5 tall. Fill and stroke are **brass** `#8C6414` (light) / `#D9A84A` (dark). The 1-unit round-join stroke in the same colour softens the corners.
4. **Proportions:** bar width : bob width = 16 : 11. The line is 13 units, the bob 10.5. The bob sits slightly below optical centre, as a weight should.
5. **Small sizes (16 px or less):** set the line stroke to 2.5, the bar height to 3.5, and drop the bob's soft stroke.
6. **App icon:** the mark centred on a cobalt `#2447C8` rounded square (rx 22% of the side). Bar and line in white, bob in `#D9A84A`.

**Wordmark:** "plumbline" in lowercase, **Instrument Sans SemiBold (600)**, tracking -0.015em. Lock-up: the mark is 1.6× the cap height, with a gap of 0.4× the mark width. No custom glyphs.

**Fonts** (all OFL, on npm):
- Display / wordmark: `@fontsource/instrument-sans` (600, 700)
- Body / UI: `@fontsource/instrument-sans` (400, 500), with `font-feature-settings: "tnum"` in tables
- Mono (commit hashes, URLs, citations): `@fontsource/jetbrains-mono` (400, 500)

#### Motion: "settle"
Things move like a plumb bob. They arrive with intent, then settle and stay still. There is at most one small overshoot, used only for the signature moment.

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--ease-out` | `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` | entrances, popovers |
| `--ease-in-out` | `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)` | panels, route changes |
| `--ease-settle` | `cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.32, 0.64, 1)` | signature only (about 4% overshoot) |
| `--dur-instant` | 90 ms | hover, press |
| `--dur-quick` | 160 ms | chips, toggles, tabs |
| `--dur-standard` | 240 ms | menus, drawers (content) |
| `--dur-deliberate` | 360 ms | side panels, page transitions |
| `--dur-signature` | 520 ms | the plumb drop |

**Signature animations**
1. **Plumb drop.** When a claim moves up a tier, the new rung fills from the top down (`clip-path: inset(0 0 100% 0)` → `inset(0)`, 520 ms, `--ease-settle`). A 1 px vertical rule then draws down the evidence column from the claim to its citation (`stroke-dashoffset`, 360 ms, `--ease-out`).
2. **Citation reveal.** Opening a source fades the citation card in and moves it 4 px up (240 ms). The quoted line in the source gets a 160 ms highlight wash in primary-subtle, which then stays as a static underline.
3. **Theme switch.** A 200 ms colour cross-fade with no movement.

### 4.2 Assay

**Pronunciation:** uh-SAY /əˈseɪ/, stressed like the verb. Say it this way consistently in all audio and video. The noun form "ASS-ay" is common in British and scientific usage, and that is the main risk with this name.

**Meaning and story.** To assay gold is to test it and find out what it really contains. Assayers streak the metal on a black touchstone and read the colour of the mark. Assay does the same with a resume: it tests what each claim is made of, cites the result, and a person signs it off.

**Tagline:** *Test every claim. Cite every source.*
Alternates: "What's in a resume, assayed." / "Proof, not polish."

**Conflict notes:** see section 3. No HR or recruiting conflict found. The name is common in life-science software (different buyers, possibly overlapping Class 42 filings). The status of assay.com is unknown and it is probably held. Short dictionary words are harder to register as marks, so expect a narrower registration (for example word plus logo).

**Domain ideas** (not checked): `assay.work`, `assayhq.com`, `tryassay.com`, `assayhiring.com`, `assay.so`.

### 4.3 Gradus

**Pronunciation:** GRAH-dus /ˈɡrɑː.dəs/. Pick one pronunciation and use it consistently. Some English speakers will say GRAY-dus (as in the classical *Gradus ad Parnassum*).

**Meaning and story.** *Gradus* is Latin for "a step", the rung of a ladder. The product is built around the Evidence Ladder, and every claim climbs it one cited step at a time: from something a candidate says, to something shown, to something confirmed in person.

**Tagline:** *Evidence, one rung at a time.*
Alternates: "Step by step, to proof." / "Climb from claim to proof."

## 5. Recommendation: Plumbline

**Why Plumbline**
1. **The metaphor carries the hardest part of the pitch.** Buyers already get "evidence". What sets this product apart is *fairness and consistency*: everyone measured against the same objective line. A plumb line is the plainest picture of that idea, and "plumb" already means *exactly* and *true* in everyday English.
2. **It works for both audiences.** Recruiters hear rigour. Candidates hear "measured fairly", not "graded". Assay can sound like candidates are being tested, and Gradus can be read as "grade us".
3. **Cleanest quick check of the three.** No conflict found in HR, identity checks or developer tools. The only software-class PLUMBLINE registration found is cancelled. Plumbing businesses are a different trade.
4. **Plain English with one easy pronunciation.** Assay's "uh-SAY" vs "ASS-ay" problem is real in sales calls and demos.
5. **Strong identity material.** A simple mark that works at favicon size, a natural motion idea ("settle"), and a calm cobalt-and-slate palette that reads as "proper professional software" without the AI-purple look.

**Known downsides, and how to handle them**
- *Plumbing SEO.* Use a two-word brand in search and ads ("Plumbline hiring", "Plumbline Evidence"), and a non-.com domain or a prefixed .com at launch.
- *The silent b.* Some people will type "plumline". If budget allows, register that misspelling as a redirect.
- *The .com is probably held* (the fetch timed out, so this was not confirmed). Don't rely on getting it.

**Runner-up:** **Assay.** It is the sharpest fit for "evidence" and gives the most distinctive monochrome identity. Choose it if the owner is comfortable with the pronunciation risk and a busy lab-software namespace. **Gradus** is the most on-concept internally (it *is* the Evidence Ladder), but its weaknesses are a buyer-facing problem.

## 6. Before adopting (any name)

1. **Formal trademark clearance** (lawyer or clearance service): USPTO TSDR, EUIPO, UKIPO and the Indian Trade Marks Registry, classes 9, 35 and 42, plus common-law and business-name searches.
2. Check domain availability and pricing through a registrar. Nothing was bought or reserved.
3. Check social handles (LinkedIn, X, GitHub organisation) and package-name squatting (npm scope, PyPI).
4. Run a quick pronunciation and associations test with 5 to 10 recruiters and 5 to 10 candidates.
5. Only then plan the rename in code (package names, page titles, emails, `RecruitPro` strings). That work is out of scope here.
